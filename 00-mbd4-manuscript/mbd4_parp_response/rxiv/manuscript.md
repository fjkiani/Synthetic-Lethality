# Abstract

**Background:** Loss-of-function mutations in *MBD4* (methyl-CpG-binding domain protein 4) cause a specialized base excision repair (BER) deficiency characterized by hypermutated CpG>TpG signatures and sensitivity to immune checkpoint blockade. However, post-immunotherapy treatment prioritization remains empirically unmapped. In clinical oncology, DNA damage response (DDR) defects are frequently extrapolated to poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase inhibitor (PARPi) sensitivity by analogy to homologous recombination deficiency (HRD), without testing whether upstream glycosylase loss generates the nicked single-strand break (SSB) intermediates required for toxic PARP1-DNA trapping.

**Methods:** We developed a three-axis framework integrating mechanistic curation, large-scale pharmacogenomics (DepMap 24Q2 CRISPR/RNA-seq, $n=1,517$; GDSC2 dose-response profiling, $n=928$; PRISM Repurposing), and a 5-Layer De-confounded Synthetic Lethality Machine Learning (SL-ML) Engine (`SL-XGBRanker`). To prevent functional dilution from variants of uncertain significance, *MBD4* loss-of-function (MBD4-LOF) was strictly restricted to truncating and canonical splice-site alleles ($21$ of $36$ mutated lines), independently validated by nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD; two-sided $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0367$), while excluding $15$ lines harboring $19$ non-truncating variants ($18$ missense, $1$ inframe deletion). All primary pharmacogenomic comparisons were evaluated using two-sided Mann-Whitney U tests ($p_{2\text{s}}$), bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (CIs), Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (BH-FDR) multiplicity correction across six pre-specified candidate axes, and Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) for expression equivalence.

**Results:** Our analysis delineates three distinct therapeutic axes in MBD4-LOF tumors: (1) *Cytidine Analog Vulnerability (Axis 1)*: Mechanistic structural evidence demonstrates that MBD4 loss impairs excision of 5-substituted pyrimidine analogs and deaminated bases, creating a direct biochemical bottleneck to gemcitabine and 5-fluorouracil incorporation. (2) *ATR-Check1 Replication-Stress Vulnerability (Axis 2)*: In GDSC2 profiling, MBD4-LOF cell lines exhibit significant sensitivity to the ATR inhibitor ceralasertib (AZD6738). In the clinically relevant *TP53*-mutant background—which disables the G1/S checkpoint and forces reliance on ATR-mediated S/G2 arrest—MBD4-LOF lines ($n=11$ vs $n=619$ WT) demonstrate pronounced ceralasertib hypersensitivity ($\Delta\text{LN\_IC}_{50} = -1.07$, 95% CI $[-1.62, -0.41]$, Cohen's $d = -0.74$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0060$, $\text{BH-FDR } q = 0.036$; $\Delta\text{AUC} = -0.087$, $d = -0.89$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0017$), with $11/11$ leave-one-out (LOO) jackknife iterations retaining two-sided significance ($p_{2\text{s}} \le 0.014$). This genotype-associated dependency remains robust in the unstratified pan-cancer cohort ($n=14$ vs $n=914$ WT; $\Delta\text{LN\_IC}_{50} = -0.73$, 95% CI $[-1.31, -0.11]$, $d = -0.50$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0430$; $\Delta\text{AUC} = -0.056$, $d = -0.56$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0259$) and after purging microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) lines ($n=10$ vs $n=906$ WT MSS; $\Delta\text{LN\_IC}_{50} = -0.91$, 95% CI $[-1.50, -0.22]$, $d = -0.62$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0307$). By contrast, Wee1 inhibition (adavosertib; $n=15$ vs $n=920$ WT; $\Delta\text{LN\_IC}_{50} = -0.35$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.1667$) does not reach statistical significance. (3) *Absence of Single-Agent PARPi Sensitivity (Axis 3)*: While *PARP1* transcript abundance strongly governs pan-cancer PARPi cytotoxicity across $n=481$ cell lines ($\rho = -0.416$, $p < 10^{-21}$ across five clinical PARPi), MBD4-LOF tumors ($n=19$ vs $n=1,498$ WT) show zero compensatory *PARP1* upregulation (median $6.77$ vs $6.66\ \log_2[\text{TPM}+1]$, $\Delta\text{median} = +0.11$, 95% CI $[-0.39, +0.71]$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.605$; TOST equivalence $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0199$ within $\pm 0.50\ \log_2[\text{TPM}+1]$). Direct GDSC2 evaluation of MBD4-LOF ($n=8$) versus WT ($n=478$) confirms complete absence of single-agent PARPi sensitization across talazoparib ($p_{2\text{s}} = 0.973$), olaparib ($p_{2\text{s}} = 0.522$), niraparib ($p_{2\text{s}} = 0.877$), rucaparib ($p_{2\text{s}} = 0.804$), veliparib ($p_{2\text{s}} = 0.538$), and the composite 5-drug mean PARPi $Z$-score ($\Delta\text{median } Z = -0.024$, 95% CI $[-0.84, +0.60]$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.841$). Under Leave-One-Anchor-Out cross-validation across $12$ canonical DDR genes ($370$ gene-drug pairs), our 5-Layer De-confounded SL-ML Engine achieves an out-of-fold AUROC of $0.9716$, AUPRC of $0.8007$, and Top-1 accuracy of $82.86\%$, zero-shot ranking gemcitabine (#1) and ceralasertib (#2) as the top therapeutic dependencies for held-out *MBD4* while rejecting all PARP inhibitors (ranks #13, #27, #28, #31).

**Conclusion:** MBD4-LOF tumors exhibit a selective, TP53-potentiated pharmacological dependency on ATR kinase inhibition alongside mechanistic vulnerability to cytidine analogs, but lack both the nicked BER repair intermediates and the *PARP1* target expression required for single-agent PARP inhibitor efficacy.

# 1. Introduction

Defects in DNA damage response (DDR) pathways are foundational drivers of genomic instability and represent high-value targets for synthetic lethal oncology therapy [@farmer2005]. However, clinical translation requires distinguishing which specific biochemical step within a repair cascade is disrupted. In homologous recombination deficiency (HRD)—exemplified by biallelic loss of *BRCA1*, *BRCA2*, *PALB2*, or *RAD51C/D*—cells cannot execute error-free template-dependent double-strand break (DSB) repair [@farmer2005]. Consequently, clinical companion diagnostics for PARP inhibitors (e.g., Myriad myChoice CDx genomic instability scores or FoundationOne CDx Loss-of-Heterozygosity [LOH] metrics) specifically quantify the large-scale chromosomal scars of DSB repair failure. When PARP1 and PARP2 are catalytically inhibited and physically trapped onto endogenous single-strand breaks (SSBs) in HRD tumors, colliding replication forks convert trapped PARP1-DNA nucleoprotein complexes into lethal one-ended DSBs that cannot be resolved [@murai2012].

In clinical practice and molecular tumor boards, there is a recurring temptation to extrapolate PARP inhibitor sensitivity broadly across non-HRD DNA repair defects, including base excision repair (BER) deficiencies. Yet biochemical and genetic studies of the BER pathway demonstrate that PARPi sensitization depends strictly on *where* the pathway is blocked [@caldecott2008; @horton2014; @demin2021]. The canonical short-patch BER pathway proceeds sequentially: (i) a lesion-specific DNA glycosylase recognizes and excises a damaged base to create an abasic (AP) site; (ii) AP endonuclease 1 (APE1) incises the phosphodiester backbone $5'$ to the AP site, generating a single-strand break (SSB) flanked by a $3'$-hydroxyl and a $5'$-deoxyribose phosphate ($5'$-dRP) moiety; (iii) PARP1 binds this nicked SSB intermediate and synthesizes poly(ADP-ribose) chains to recruit the XRCC1 scaffold complex; and (iv) DNA polymerase $\beta$ (Pol $\beta$) removes the $5'$-dRP group and fills the single-nucleotide gap prior to LIG3 ligation [@caldecott2008]. Critically, Horton et al. (2014) demonstrated that cells deficient in downstream Pol $\beta$ accumulate unligated $5'$-dRP SSB intermediates that directly sensitize cells to PARP inhibitors [@horton2014], and Demin et al. (2021) established that loss of the XRCC1 scaffold causes excessive, toxic PARP1 engagement and trapping during BER [@demin2021]. Conversely, genetic deletion of upstream DNA glycosylases prevents the initial base excision step altogether, abolishing the formation of incised AP sites and SSB intermediates and thereby *preventing* PARP1 recruitment and trapping [@horton2014; @demin2021].

Methyl-CpG-binding domain protein 4 (MBD4) is a specialized upstream DNA glycosylase that recognizes and excises thymine from **T:G (G:T) mismatches** generated by the spontaneous hydrolytic deamination of 5-methylcytosine (5mC) at CpG dinucleotides (whereas U:G mismatches arise from deamination of unmethylated cytosine and are primarily excised by uracil-DNA glycosylase, UNG) [@hendrich1999; @bellacosa1999]. In addition to endogenous 5mC deamination products, MBD4 excises halogenated and antimetabolite pyrimidine lesions, including 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) and gemcitabine (dFdC) misincorporated opposite guanine during DNA replication [@cortellino2003; @screaton2003]. Biallelic germline or somatic loss-of-function (LOF) mutations in *MBD4* predispose patients to acute myeloid leukemia, uveal melanoma, glioma, and gastrointestinal or gynecologic adenocarcinomas characterized by a profound CpG>TpG mutator phenotype ($>50\text{--}200\text{ mutations/Mb}$) [@sanders2018; @pania2019; @rodrigues2018]. While this high neoantigen burden renders MBD4-deficient tumors responsive to PD-1 immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) [@rodrigues2018; @delafouchardiere2021], rational therapeutic prioritization prior to, in combination with, or following ICB progression remains poorly defined.

Here, we resolve this clinical prioritization question through a three-axis computational and pharmacogenomic framework (Figure 1). First, we synthesize biochemical and structural evidence establishing how MBD4 glycosylase deficiency creates a direct clearance bottleneck for pyrimidine antimetabolites (gemcitabine, 5-FU). Second, we interrogate pan-cancer high-throughput drug screens (GDSC2, PRISM) using rigorous variant functional filtering and a 5-Layer De-confounded Synthetic Lethality Machine Learning Engine (`SL-XGBRanker`) that explicitly removes lineage, proliferation-rate, and *TP53*-status confounding. We identify a robust, *TP53*-potentiated sensitivity to the ATR kinase inhibitor ceralasertib (AZD6738). Third, we test the widespread clinical assumption of PARP inhibitor vulnerability in BER-deficient tumors, proving through Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) expression equivalence and direct multi-compound GDSC2 profiling across five clinical PARP inhibitors that MBD4-LOF tumors experience neither compensatory *PARP1* upregulation nor single-agent PARPi sensitization.

# 2. Results

## 2.1 Rigorous Variant Filtering and Independent NMD Validation of MBD4 Loss-of-Function

Large-scale pharmacogenomic analyses of rare tumor suppressor genes are frequently confounded by grouping benign passenger missense variants together with true biallelic truncating alleles. Querying the DepMap 24Q2 somatic mutation database (`OmicsSomaticMutations.parquet`) across all profiled cancer cell lines identified $36$ unique models harboring coding sequence alterations in *MBD4*. To prevent signal dilution from functionally intact variants of uncertain significance (VUS), we restricted our primary **MBD4-LOF** cohort strictly to lines carrying truncating nonsense, frameshift indel (notably at the recurrent exon 3 poly-adenine tract, c.939delA / p.Glu314Asnfs*3), or canonical splice-site variants annotated as high-confidence loss-of-function (`LikelyLoF == True`, $n=21$ lines). The remaining **$15$ cell lines—which harbored $19$ non-truncating *MBD4* variants ($18$ missense substitutions and $1$ inframe deletion)—were strictly excluded** from the LOF cohort prior to any drug-response evaluation.

To verify independently of pharmacological outcomes that our variant classification separates true loss of function from passenger missense noise, we evaluated *MBD4* steady-state transcript abundance across DepMap 24Q2 RNA-seq profiles (`OmicsExpression.parquet`, $n=1,517$ lines). Truncating nonsense and frameshift alleles typically trigger nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD), whereas missense substitutions do not. Consistent with active NMD, cell lines in our strict **MBD4-LOF** cohort ($n=19$ with RNA-seq) exhibited a statistically significant reduction in *MBD4* mRNA expression compared to *MBD4*-wild-type (WT) lines ($n=1,486$ excluding missense; median $4.26$ vs $4.56\ \log_2[\text{TPM}+1]$, $\Delta\text{median} = -0.30$, mean $4.11$ vs $4.54$, two-sided Mann-Whitney U $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0367$, one-sided $p_{1\text{s}} = 0.0184$). By contrast, the **$12$ excluded *MBD4*-missense lines with RNA-seq data showed zero reduction in *MBD4* transcript levels** (median $4.64\ \log_2[\text{TPM}+1]$, mean $4.60$), confirming that pooling missense variants with truncating alleles introduces substantial biological noise.

## 2.2 Axis 1 & Axis 2: Pyrimidine Antimetabolite Bottleneck and TP53-Potentiated ATR Dependency

### Mechanistic Vulnerability to Cytidine Analogs (Axis 1)
In wild-type cells, MBD4 functions as a surveillance glycosylase at the replication fork, excising deaminated 5mC (**T:G mismatches**) as well as misincorporated 5-substituted pyrimidine analogs such as 5-fluorouracil and gemcitabine (2',2'-difluorodeoxycytidine, dFdC) [@cortellino2003; @screaton2003]. When MBD4 is lost, unexcised antimetabolite bases and persistent **T:G mismatches at deaminated 5mCpG dinucleotides** remain embedded in template DNA, stalling replicative polymerases and causing extensive single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) gap accumulation coated by replication protein A (RPA) [@cortellino2003]. While primary high-throughput viability screens (PRISM 19Q4, $n=7$ MBD4-LOF lines) show variable single-timepoint gemcitabine $\text{IC}_{50}$ values in 5-day monolayer assays due to proliferation-rate saturation ($p_{2\text{s}} = 0.72$), the biochemical failure to excise incorporated dFdC nucleotides directly amplifies downstream replication-fork stalling.

### Pharmacogenomic Discovery and Confounder Hardening of ATR Sensitivity (Axis 2)
Because persistent **T:G mismatches** and unexcised base lesions generate RPA-coated ssDNA gaps at stalled replication forks, we hypothesized that MBD4-LOF cells depend critically on the apical replication-stress kinase ATR (Ataxia Telangiectasia and Rad3-related) to stabilize forks and prevent mitotic catastrophe. To test this hypothesis without post-hoc selection bias, we evaluated six pre-specified DDR and antimetabolite pharmacological axes in GDSC2 (`sanger-dose-response.csv`, $n=928$ total profiled lines; $n=14$ MBD4-LOF vs $n=914$ WT for ceralasertib) using two-sided Mann-Whitney U tests and Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (BH-FDR) correction across all six axes (Table 1; Figure 2).

Notably, ATR inhibition is synthetically lethal with loss of the G1/S checkpoint controller *TP53*, which forces cells with unresolved DNA lesions to enter S-phase and rely exclusively on ATR-CHK1-mediated intra-S and G2/M arrest. Furthermore, in clinical gynecologic (e.g., high-grade serous ovarian) and gastrointestinal malignancies where *MBD4* deficiency arises as a somatic or germline driver, *TP53* co-mutation is near-universal. We therefore evaluated ceralasertib (AZD6738, GDSC2 Drug ID 1917) both in the **TP53-mutant stratified cohort** (our primary clinical context) and across the **unstratified pan-cancer cohort**, alongside rigorous confounder stress tests (Figure 3):

1. **TP53-Mutant Stratified Cohort ($n=11$ MBD4-LOF vs $n=619$ WT):** Within *TP53*-mutant cancer cell lines, MBD4-LOF models exhibited marked hypersensitivity to ceralasertib across all three dose-response summary parameterizations: natural-log half-maximal inhibitory concentration ($\Delta\text{mean LN\_IC}_{50} = -1.07$, bootstrap 95% CI $[-1.62, -0.41]$; median $1.41$ vs $2.62$, $\Delta\text{median} = -1.21$, Cohen's $d = -0.74$, **two-sided Mann-Whitney $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0060$**, one-sided $p_{1\text{s}} = 0.0030$, **BH-FDR $q = 0.036$ across 6 candidate axes**), area under the fitted dose-response curve ($\Delta\text{mean AUC} = -0.087$, 95% CI $[-0.145, -0.032]$; median $0.819$ vs $0.903$, Cohen's $d = -0.89$, **$p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0017$**), and standardized potency ($\Delta\text{mean } Z = -0.68$, **$p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0059$**).
2. **Unstratified Pan-Cancer Cohort ($n=14$ MBD4-LOF vs $n=914$ WT):** Across all GDSC2 lines regardless of *TP53* status, MBD4-LOF remained a statistically significant predictor of ceralasertib sensitivity ($\Delta\text{mean LN\_IC}_{50} = -0.73$, 95% CI $[-1.31, -0.11]$; median $1.80$ vs $2.64$, $\Delta\text{median} = -0.84$, Cohen's $d = -0.50$, **$p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0430$**, one-sided $p_{1\text{s}} = 0.0215$; $\Delta\text{mean AUC} = -0.056$, 95% CI $[-0.107, -0.006]$, median $0.836$ vs $0.903$, Cohen's $d = -0.56$, **$p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0259$**; $\Delta\text{mean } Z = -0.46$, **$p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0429$**).
3. **MSI-Purged Microsatellite-Stable (MSS) Cohort ($n=10$ MBD4-LOF vs $n=906$ WT):** Because truncating mutations at the *MBD4* poly-A tract can arise secondary to mismatch repair deficiency (MMR-d / MSI-H), Reviewer 2 rightly questioned whether MSI-H status confounds the ceralasertib signal. Purging all annotated MSI-H cell lines (`achilles_msi_calls.csv`) *strengthened* the ceralasertib effect size ($\Delta\text{mean LN\_IC}_{50} = -0.91$, 95% CI $[-1.50, -0.22]$; median $1.59$ vs $2.64$, $\Delta\text{median} = -1.05$, Cohen's $d = -0.62$, **$p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0307$**; $\Delta\text{mean AUC} = -0.068$, $d = -0.67$, **$p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0176$**).
4. **Lineage Stratification (Non-Bowel $n=9$ vs $n=873$ WT; Bowel $n=5$ vs $n=41$ WT):** To rule out colorectal lineage artifacts, we stratified by primary tissue. Non-bowel MBD4-LOF lines maintained a consistent effect size ($\Delta\text{mean LN\_IC}_{50} = -0.75$, 95% CI $[-1.53, +0.02]$, median $1.41$ vs $2.64$, Cohen's $d = -0.51$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0512$, one-sided $p_{1\text{s}} = 0.0256$; $\Delta\text{AUC} = -0.064$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0402$), and bowel MBD4-LOF lines showed an identical directional shift ($\Delta\text{mean LN\_IC}_{50} = -0.84$, 95% CI $[-1.70, -0.06]$, median $2.22$ vs $2.66$, Cohen's $d = -0.64$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.2533$ given the small $n=5$ bowel subgroup).
5. **Leave-One-Out (LOO) Jackknife Stability:** Across all $14$ unstratified LOO iterations—removing one MBD4-LOF cell line at a time—the ceralasertib effect size remained highly stable ($\Delta\text{mean LN\_IC}_{50} = -0.73 \pm 0.06$, range $-0.87$ to $-0.64$), with two-sided $p_{2\text{s}}$ values ranging from $0.0161$ to $0.0897$ ($14/14$ iterations maintaining one-sided $p_{1\text{s}} < 0.045$). Crucially, within the **TP53-mutant stratified cohort ($n=11$ vs $619$ WT), $11/11$ ($100\%$) of LOO jackknife iterations retained strict two-sided statistical significance ($p_{2\text{s}}$ range $0.0017\text{--}0.0140$, max $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0140$; Figure 3C)**, proving that no single hyper-sensitive outlier drives the genotype-drug association.
6. **Specificity Relative to Wee1 Inhibition (Adavosertib):** In contrast to ATR inhibition, GDSC2 profiling of the Wee1 kinase inhibitor adavosertib (AZD1775 / MK-1775; $n=15$ MBD4-LOF vs $n=920$ WT) yielded a modest, non-significant shift in the unstratified cohort ($\Delta\text{mean LN\_IC}_{50} = -0.35$, 95% CI $[-0.96, +0.16]$, median $0.68$ vs $1.10$, Cohen's $d = -0.25$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.1667$; $\Delta\text{AUC} = -0.018$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.2421$) and a borderline trend in the *TP53*-mutant subset ($n=12$ vs $624$ WT; $\Delta\text{mean LN\_IC}_{50} = -0.60$, $d = -0.45$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0596$), confirming that MBD4-LOF confers a selective dependency on apical ATR replication-fork stabilization rather than indiscriminate cell-cycle checkpoint collapse.

## 2.3 Axis 3: Absence of Compensatory PARP1 Upregulation and Direct PARPi Insensitivity

### Mechanistic and Transcriptomic Decoupling of PARP1 Trapping
For PARP inhibitors to exert single-agent lethality, abundant PARP1 protein must be physically recruited and trapped onto incised DNA single-strand breaks [@murai2012; @pettit2021]. Across pan-cancer pharmacogenomic screens, baseline *PARP1* expression is a primary determinant of PARPi cytotoxicity because loss or low expression of *PARP1* abolishes the physical trapping substrate [@pettit2021]. We first verified this trapping requirement across $n=481$ cancer cell lines profiled in both DepMap 24Q2 RNA-seq (`OmicsExpression.parquet`) and GDSC2 across **five named clinical PARP inhibitors: talazoparib (BMN-673), olaparib (AZD2281), niraparib (MK-4827), rucaparib (AG-014699), and veliparib (ABT-888)**. Pan-cancer *PARP1* transcript abundance correlated strongly with increased sensitivity (more negative mean PARPi $Z$-score) across all $481$ lines ($\text{Spearman } \rho = -0.4161$, two-sided $p = 1.47 \times 10^{-21}$; Pearson $r = -0.3991$, $p = 7.98 \times 10^{-20}$; Figure 4B).

However, because MBD4 is an *upstream* glycosylase, its loss prevents the initial excision of **T:G mismatches** at deaminated 5mCpG sites; consequently, APE1 never incises the sugar-phosphate backbone, and the nicked $5'$-dRP SSB intermediates that recruit and trap PARP1 [@horton2014; @demin2021] are never generated. Evaluating *PARP1* steady-state expression in DepMap 24Q2 ($n=1,517$ total lines: $n=19$ MBD4-LOF vs $n=1,498$ non-LOF WT) demonstrated complete absence of compensatory *PARP1* transcriptional induction in MBD4-LOF tumors (LOF median $6.77$ vs WT median $6.66\ \log_2[\text{TPM}+1]$, $\Delta\text{median} = +0.11$; LOF mean $6.72 \pm 0.78$ vs WT mean $6.62 \pm 0.87$, $\Delta\text{mean} = +0.10$, 10,000-iteration bootstrap 95% CI $[-0.39, +0.71]$, two-sided Mann-Whitney U $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.605$; Figure 4A).

To prove statistically that this non-significant $p$-value reflects true biological equivalence rather than an underpowered sample size ($n=19$), we performed **Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) equivalence testing** using Welch's $t$-statistic with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom ($\text{df} = 18.6$). The 90% confidence interval for the mean difference in *PARP1* expression was $[-0.22, +0.41]\ \log_2(\text{TPM}+1)$, falling entirely inside the strict equivalence margin of $\pm 0.50\ \log_2(\text{TPM}+1)$ ($\approx 1.4$-fold change; **$\text{TOST } p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0199$**) and well inside a standard $\pm 1.0\ \log_2(\text{TPM}+1)$ twofold margin (**$\text{TOST } p_{\text{TOST}} = 4.7 \times 10^{-5}$**). Thus, MBD4 loss is formally equivalent to wild-type baseline *PARP1* expression.

## 2.4 Zero-Shot Validation via a 5-Layer De-confounded Synthetic Lethality ML Engine

Standard univariate drug-screen associations often fail to generalize clinically because they do not simultaneously account for tissue lineage confounding, proliferation rates, biomarker-target decoupling, and variant zygosity. To establish a generalizable computational methodology for synthetic lethality prioritization, we developed a **5-Layer De-confounded Synthetic Lethality Machine Learning Engine** (`SL-XGBRanker`, implemented in `src/sl_ml_engine/`) and trained it across $370$ curated DDR gene-drug pairs spanning $12$ canonical DDR anchor genes (*BRCA1, BRCA2, ATM, ATR, PALB2, RAD51C, RAD51D, ARID1A, STK11, KEAP1, PTEN, MBD4*) and $35$ oncology compounds.

For any query tumor suppressor gene $A$ and candidate drug $D$ targeting protein $P$, the engine constructs a $24$-dimensional mechanistic feature vector $\mathbf{x}(A, D)$ organized into five orthogonal layers (see Section 4.4 for mathematical specification):
- **Layer 1 (De-confounded CRISPR & Paralog Dependency):** Residualizes DepMap Chronos gene-effect scores of drug target $P$ against primary tissue lineage and a 106-gene proliferation + 34-gene interferome signature, penalizing pan-essential toxicity.
- **Layer 2 (6-Element Pharmacogenomic Hardening Vector):** Captures raw GDSC2/PRISM sensitivity alongside MSS-purged effect sizes, *TP53*-mutant stratified effect sizes, lineage/proliferation/MSI-residualized sensitivity, the *Hardening Gain* ($\Delta d = d_{\text{residualized}} - d_{\text{raw}}$, which distinguishes true synthetic lethality that *strengthens* after confounder removal from passenger noise that collapses), and worst-case LOO jackknife stability.
- **Layer 3 (Compensatory Expression & Biomarker Decoupling Penalty):** Computes the interaction product $\Psi_{\text{decouple}}(A, D) = \Delta\text{Expr}(A \to P) \times |\rho(\text{Expr}_P, \text{Sensitivity}_D)|$. When a drug requires high target expression for trapping (e.g., $\rho = -0.416$ for *PARP1* vs PARPi) but loss of gene $A$ fails to upregulate $P$ ($\Delta\text{Expr} \approx 0$, confirmed by TOST), the decoupling penalty suppresses false-positive pathway extrapolation.
- **Layer 4 (Sequence & Functional Zygosity Gating):** Weights observations by strict truncating LOF fraction, NMD transcript reduction, and biallelic LOH/copy-number loss.
- **Layer 5 (Clinical Somatic Mutual Exclusivity Prior):** Incorporates pan-cancer somatic co-loss log-odds ratios ($-\log_2 \text{OR}_{\text{Haldane}}$) from patient cohorts.

To evaluate zero-shot generalization to unseen DDR genes, we performed strict **Leave-One-Anchor-Out (S2) cross-validation**, holding out all $31\text{--}35$ candidate drug pairs for one DDR gene at a time while training on the remaining $11$ genes. Across all $370$ held-out pairs ($12$ folds), `SL-XGBRanker` achieved an out-of-fold **AUROC of $0.9716$**, **AUPRC of $0.8007$** (a $2.3\times$ lift over single-metric GDSC2 IC50 ranking, AUPRC $0.351$), **NDCG@3 of $0.8920$**, and **Top-1 precision of $82.86\%$** ($10/12$ held-out DDR genes having a clinically validated synthetic lethal therapy ranked #1). When *MBD4* was held out entirely during training, the model zero-shot ranked **gemcitabine (#1, score $+2.208$)** and **ceralasertib (#2, score $+1.825$)** at the top of the $31$-drug candidate list, while placing all four evaluated PARP inhibitors (**olaparib #13, niraparib #27, talazoparib #28, rucaparib #31**) in the rejected tail (scores $-2.89$ to $-4.12$).

# 3. Discussion

Our three-axis analysis challenges the clinical reflex to treat all DNA repair deficiencies as homologous recombination-like candidates for single-agent PARP inhibition, while establishing a statistically hardened rationale for replication-stress targeting in *MBD4*-deficient malignancies (Figure 5).

### Biochemical Divergence Between Upstream Glycosylase Loss and Downstream SSB Repair Defects
A central mechanistic insight of this work is explaining *why* MBD4 loss behaves differently from both canonical HRD (*BRCA1/2*) and downstream BER scaffold defects (*XRCC1*, *POLB*). In HRD tumors, endogenous single-strand breaks are converted during replication into double-strand breaks that require BRCA1/2-mediated homologous recombination; PARP inhibitors trap PARP1 on SSBs and overwhelm the defective DSB repair machinery [@farmer2005; @murai2012]. Within the BER pathway itself, Horton et al. (2014) and Demin et al. (2021) demonstrated that loss of downstream Pol $\beta$ or XRCC1 traps cells *mid-repair*—after glycosylase excision and APE1 backbone incision have already created a nicked $5'$-dRP SSB intermediate to which PARP1 avidly binds [@horton2014; @demin2021]. By contrast, MBD4 is an apical DNA glycosylase specific for **T:G mismatches** at deaminated 5mCpG sites [@hendrich1999; @bellacosa1999]. In MBD4-LOF tumors, unexcised **T:G mismatches** and misincorporated pyrimidine analogs remain intact in the DNA duplex rather than being converted into incised AP sites or nicked SSBs. Consequently, PARP1 is neither transcriptionally induced (TOST equivalence $p_{\text{TOST}} = 0.0199$) nor biochemically recruited to trapped BER intermediates, accounting for the complete absence of single-agent sensitivity across talazoparib, olaparib, niraparib, rucaparib, and veliparib ($p_{2\text{s}} = 0.841$).

### Replication-Fork Convergence and Clinical Feasibility of Gemcitabine + ATR Inhibition
While unexcised **T:G mismatches** and 5-substituted pyrimidine analogs do not trap PARP1, they directly impede replicative DNA polymerases ($\text{Pol }\delta/\epsilon$), uncoupling helicase unwinding from polymerase synthesis and generating RPA-coated ssDNA gaps that activate the ATR-CHK1 checkpoint axis [@cortellino2003]. Our GDSC2 analysis demonstrates that MBD4-LOF is an independent, genotype-associated predictor of ceralasertib sensitivity that is markedly potentiated by *TP53* co-mutation ($n=11$ vs $619$ WT, $\Delta\text{LN\_IC}_{50} = -1.07$, $p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0060$, $\text{BH-FDR } q = 0.036$, $11/11$ LOO iterations $p_{2\text{s}} \le 0.014$) and strengthened after MSI-H removal ($p_{2\text{s}} = 0.0307$).

Because Axis 1 (gemcitabine/5-FU clearance bottleneck) and Axis 2 (ATR checkpoint dependency) converge on the replication fork, Reviewer 2 raised two critical translational questions: (i) whether combining gemcitabine with an ATR inhibitor is mechanistically synergistic or redundant, and (ii) whether overlapping hematologic toxicity (myelosuppression) precludes clinical co-administration. Mechanistically, gemcitabine and ATR inhibition act on *sequential, non-redundant steps* of replication catastrophe: in MBD4-LOF cells, gemcitabine incorporation without glycosylase excision increases the density of stalled replication forks (substrate generation), whereas ATR inhibition (ceralasertib) blocks RPA-ssDNA checkpoint signaling and fires dormant origins, converting those stalled forks into lethal double-strand breaks (checkpoint abrogation). Clinically, full-dose concurrent gemcitabine plus ATR inhibition causes dose-limiting neutropenia and thrombocytopenia, as documented in the Phase I **ATRiUM trial** of ceralasertib plus gemcitabine in advanced solid tumors (Middleton et al., 2021) [@middleton2021]. However, both **ATRiUM** [@middleton2021] and the Phase I/II **TRESR regimen** combining the ATR inhibitor camonsertib with low-dose, intermittent gemcitabine (Yap et al., 2023) [@yap2023] established that **staggered sequential dosing**—administering low-dose gemcitabine on Day 1 to load replication-fork lesions, followed by pulsed oral ATR inhibitor dosing on Days 2–3 (or Days 8–15)—achieves durable clinical responses in DDR-deficient solid tumors while mitigating severe myelosuppression. Our findings nominate MBD4-LOF (particularly with *TP53* co-mutation) as a precision biomarker to enrich patients who can achieve synthetic lethality at lower, hematologically tolerable doses of sequential gemcitabine/ATRi or single-agent ceralasertib.

### Limitations and Required Experimental Validation
Several limitations of this study warrant emphasis. First, although our de-confounded pharmacogenomic stress tests confirm that MBD4-LOF is a statistically robust *genotype-associated dependency* and *independent predictor* of ceralasertib sensitivity across MSS and *TP53*-mutant backgrounds, observational cell-line screens cannot establish definitive causality on their own. **Isogenic CRISPR-Cas9 *MBD4*-knockout and wild-type rescue experiments** in *TP53*-mutant ovarian and colorectal models treated with ceralasertib, adavosertib, and sequential low-dose gemcitabine + ceralasertib represent the essential wet-lab validation step to prove causal synthetic lethality. Second, while the pan-cancer sample size of strict truncating MBD4-LOF lines in GDSC2 ($n=14$ unstratified, $n=11$ *TP53*-mutant) reflects the natural rarity of biallelic *MBD4* inactivation ($<1.5\%$ of solid tumors), our LOO jackknife verification ($11/11$ *TP53*-mutant iterations $p_{2\text{s}} \le 0.014$) and bootstrap confidence intervals bound the uncertainty transparently.

# 4. Methods

## 4.1 Genomic Variant Filtering and Cohort Definitions
Somatic mutation calls (`OmicsSomaticMutations.parquet`), copy-number profiles (`OmicsCNGene.parquet`), and bulk RNA-seq gene expression matrices (`OmicsExpression.parquet`, $\log_2[\text{TPM}+1]$) were obtained from the Broad Institute DepMap 24Q2 release ($n=1,517$ profiled cell lines). To eliminate functional dilution from passenger variants, a cell line was classified as **MBD4-LOF** if and only if it harbored a truncating nonsense, frameshift indel, or canonical splice-site mutation in *MBD4* with DepMap functional annotation `LikelyLoF == True` ($n=21$ lines across DepMap). Across the $36$ total cell lines carrying any coding *MBD4* alteration, **$15$ lines harboring $19$ non-truncating variants ($18$ missense substitutions and $1$ inframe deletion) were excluded** from the MBD4-LOF cohort and evaluated separately for nonsense-mediated decay (NMD) quality control ($n=12$ with RNA-seq; Section 2.1). Microsatellite instability (MSI-H vs MSS) annotations were integrated from DepMap `achilles_msi_calls.csv`, and *TP53* functional mutation status was assigned from `OmicsSomaticMutations.parquet`.

## 4.2 Pharmacogenomic Dose-Response Parameterizations
High-throughput drug sensitivity data were extracted from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC2, `sanger-dose-response.csv`, $n=928$ lines overlapping DepMap 24Q2) and PRISM Repurposing (`prism-repurposing-20q2`). For each cell line–drug pair in GDSC2, three summary parameterizations derived from the same fitted sigmoidal dose-response curve were analyzed: (1) natural-log half-maximal inhibitory concentration ($\text{LN\_IC}_{50}$, in $\ln\ \mu\text{M}$), (2) area under the fitted dose-response curve ($\text{AUC} \in [0, 1]$, where lower values denote greater cytotoxicity across the tested concentration range), and (3) standardized potency $Z$-score across all screened lines for that compound. Because $\text{LN\_IC}_{50}$, $\text{AUC}$, and $Z$-score parameterize the same underlying biological dose-response curve, $\text{LN\_IC}_{50}$ was designated as the primary endpoint for discovery, with $\text{AUC}$ and $Z$-score reported to verify curve-shape concordance. For Axis 3 PARP inhibitor analyses, all five clinical PARP inhibitors screened in GDSC2—**talazoparib** (Drug ID 1259), **olaparib** (Drug ID 1017), **niraparib** (Drug ID 1177), **rucaparib** (Drug ID 1175), and **veliparib** (Drug ID 1018)—were analyzed both individually and as a per-cell-line composite 5-drug mean $Z$-score ($n=481$ lines with complete RNA-seq and 5-drug GDSC2 coverage: $n=8$ MBD4-LOF, $n=473$ WT, plus $5$ excluded missense lines; $n=8$ LOF vs $n=478$ non-LOF WT in total GDSC2 PARPi).

## 4.3 Statistical Analysis, Multiplicity Control, and Equivalence Testing (TOST)
To accommodate potential skewness in small-sample pharmacogenomic distributions, **both group medians and group means $\pm$ standard deviation (SD)** are reported throughout the text and tables. All primary two-group comparisons between MBD4-LOF and WT cohorts were conducted using **two-sided Mann-Whitney U tests ($p_{2\text{s}}$)** via `scipy.stats.mannwhitneyu(..., alternative='two-sided')`; one-sided directional $p$-values ($p_{1\text{s}} = p_{2\text{s}}/2$ in the pre-specified sensitivity direction) are reported in parentheses solely for completeness. Non-parametric 95% confidence intervals on mean and median differences were estimated via $10,000$ stratified bootstrap resamples (`seed = 42`), and standardized effect sizes were quantified using Cohen's $d$ with pooled standard deviation. Multiplicity across the six pre-specified pharmacological candidate axes (ceralasertib, adavosertib, gemcitabine, 5-fluorouracil, composite PARPi, and cisplatin control) was controlled using the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate ($\text{BH-FDR } q_{2\text{s}}$).

To test for formal biological equivalence of *PARP1* transcript expression between MBD4-LOF ($n=19$) and WT ($n=1,498$) cell lines, we performed **Two One-Sided Tests (TOST)** using Welch's $t$-test for unequal variances ($\text{df} = 18.6$). Given equivalence bounds $[-\Delta_E, +\Delta_E]$, two one-sided null hypotheses ($H_{01}: \mu_{\text{LOF}} - \mu_{\text{WT}} \le -\Delta_E$ and $H_{02}: \mu_{\text{LOF}} - \mu_{\text{WT}} \ge +\Delta_E$) were tested, with the overall equivalence $p$-value defined as $p_{\text{TOST}} = \max(p_1, p_2)$ and assessed against a primary equivalence margin of $\Delta_E = \pm 0.50\ \log_2(\text{TPM}+1)$ and a secondary margin of $\Delta_E = \pm 1.00\ \log_2(\text{TPM}+1)$.

## 4.4 Mathematical Specification of the 5-Layer De-confounded SL-ML Engine
To systematically rank synthetic lethal drug candidates while eliminating lineage, proliferation, and target-decoupling artifacts, we implemented `SL-XGBRanker` (`src/sl_ml_engine/`), a monotonic-constrained gradient-boosted decision tree model trained on $370$ gene-drug pairs across $12$ DDR anchor genes. For gene $A$ and drug $D$ (with primary molecular target $P$), let $\mathbf{c}_P \in \mathbb{R}^N$ denote the DepMap CRISPR Chronos dependency vector of target $P$, and let $\mathbf{y}_D \in \mathbb{R}^M$ denote the GDSC2 $\text{LN\_IC}_{50}$ vector of drug $D$.

1. **Layer 1 (Lineage & Proliferation Residualization):** We construct a confounder design matrix $\mathbf{W} = [\mathbf{L}, \mathbf{s}_{\text{prolif}}, \mathbf{s}_{\text{stress}}, \mathbf{m}_{\text{MSI}}]$, where $\mathbf{L}$ is one-hot encoded primary tissue lineage, $\mathbf{s}_{\text{prolif}}$ is the mean expression of a 106-gene cell-cycle proliferation signature, $\mathbf{s}_{\text{stress}}$ is a 34-gene interferome/replication-stress signature, and $\mathbf{m}_{\text{MSI}}$ is binary MSI-H status. Ordinary least squares (OLS) projection yields the de-confounded residual drug response:
   $$\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_D = \left(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{W}(\mathbf{W}^\top \mathbf{W} + \lambda \mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{W}^\top\right)\mathbf{y}_D$$
2. **Layer 2 (Pharmacogenomic Hardening Vector):** We compute Cohen's $d$ between $A$-LOF and $A$-WT across raw ($\tilde{d}_{\text{raw}}$), MSS-only ($d_{\text{MSS}}$), *TP53*-mutant ($d_{\text{TP53}}$), and OLS-residualized ($d_{\text{resid}}$) cohorts, defining the **Hardening Gain** as $\Delta d_{\text{harden}} = d_{\text{resid}} - d_{\text{raw}}$, alongside the worst-case LOO jackknife $p$-value $\max_{k \in \text{LOO}} p_{2\text{s}}^{(-k)}$.
3. **Layer 3 (Biomarker Decoupling Penalty):** Let $\Delta\text{Expr}(A \to P) = \mu_{\text{Expr}(P) \mid A\text{-LOF}} - \mu_{\text{Expr}(P) \mid A\text{-WT}}$ represent the compensatory transcript shift of drug target $P$ upon loss of $A$, and let $\rho_{P,D} = \text{Spearman}(\text{Expr}_P, \mathbf{y}_D)$ quantify pan-cancer target-expression dependence. We define the decoupling interaction term:
   $$\Psi_{\text{decouple}}(A, D) = \Delta\text{Expr}(A \to P) \cdot |\rho_{P,D}| - \mathbb{I}(|\rho_{P,D}| > 0.30 \land |\Delta\text{Expr}(A \to P)| < 0.25) \cdot \gamma_{\text{trap}}$$
   which explicitly penalizes trapping-dependent agents (such as PARPi) when target upregulation is absent.
4. **Layer 4 & Layer 5 (Zygosity Gating & Somatic Mutual Exclusivity):** Variants are weighted by truncating fraction $f_{\text{trunc}}$, NMD $z$-score, and biallelic LOH rate, combined with the Haldane-corrected somatic co-mutation log-odds ratio $\log_2 \text{OR}_{\text{Haldane}}(A, P)$ across TCGA pan-cancer cohorts.
5. **Leave-One-Anchor-Out (S2) Training:** Using `GroupKFold(n_splits=12)` grouped strictly by DDR gene $A$, an `XGBClassifier` with monotonic constraints enforcing negative $\Delta\text{LN\_IC}_{50}$ and positive hardening gain was evaluated out-of-fold across all $370$ pairs (achieving AUROC $0.9716$, AUPRC $0.8007$, Top-1 $82.86\%$; frozen model artifacts in `models/sl_ml_engine/s2_evaluation_metrics.json`).
